# Training-transfer experience and proposed service

Updated October 5, 2026. Working business name: **Training to Results**; final name remains open.

## Initial buyer

An owner or operations leader at a company with about **50 employees**, one HR generalist, and perhaps a recruiter or designated trainer. The company has limited training infrastructure and one painful performance problem it has not resolved.

## Promise and scope

Work with the client's subject matter expert to define successful work, diagnose the obstacle, and implement one measurable improvement. Use focused training when the problem calls for it, supported by real-work practice, job aids, supervisor feedback, and follow-up. Select a costly problem that is also feasible to address with the available expertise and authority.

The **$1,450 diagnostic is a pricing hypothesis**, followed by a separately scoped implementation project. This is a bounded engagement. A client expert supplies the domain knowledge and validates the standard; Charles supplies diagnosis, training design, practice and assessment, and measurement. AI-assisted capture and drafting may reduce production effort, but that benefit has not yet been measured.

## Recruiting case — founder-reported

Charles describes spending two days mapping recruiting processes, identifying sourcing qualified candidates as the bottleneck, and developing a six-hour program. In the first pilot, six recruiters used live requisitions that lacked candidates, practiced each step, and completed a one-hour assessment to source five additional qualified candidates. **He reports all six achieved the objective.** He reports successful subsequent in-person and online delivery; later cohort counts are not established here.

This is historical evidence of performance on real work. The employer, dates, qualification standard, original records, durability, and hiring or revenue outcomes need confirmation. Do not infer 30 unique candidates, guaranteed hires, or results for every client.

## Doxa case — founder-reported, with supporting historical materials

Charles worked with two founders who understood the painting business's work processes. They codified successful work and used it to develop interview assessments, limited competency training/checks, and an ongoing training approach. A founder then developed critical-skill job sheets and standards. At the time, the company had about eight to ten employees, according to Charles; this is smaller than the new target.

This is an experience example of the subject matter expert collaboration model. It was employee work, not a validated external fixed-price engagement. Retrieved documents and email summaries support collaboration on painter standards, skills matrices, operational reporting, and recruiting processes, but do not quantify attributable business improvement. Charles reports that Doxa was later acquired and Coby took a leadership role at the acquiring company. The evidence does not establish that the training work caused the acquisition.
